# The neighbours

**Outcome: you can place Synchronet among
the other ways people run small text
communities — the surviving BBS packages,
and the web tools that inherited the
audience — and say what each would give
you that this one does not, and the
reverse.**

None of these were run in this guide's
lab; they are characterised from their
projects' documentation and reputations,
and the text claims no more than that.

## The other BBS packages

- **Mystic BBS** — the other widely run
  modern BBS package: free, actively
  developed, with its own scripting
  language and a strong door-game and
  network scene. Callers would struggle
  to say which they had logged into;
  sysops choose on configuration style
  and community. If Synchronet's
  text-file configuration appeals, you
  are already on the side of that choice.
- **WWIV** — one of the original 1980s
  BBS programs, still maintained as open
  source, with its own network heritage.
  Closer to a restoration than a rebuild:
  choose it to run the actual historical
  software, accept its era's limits.
- **ENiGMA½** — a modern BBS written in
  Node.js, built for the ANSI-art and
  demo-scene side of the hobby: beautiful
  menus, message networks, a younger
  codebase. Choose it if the art scene is
  your community and JavaScript is your
  native tongue.
- **The originals, under emulation** —
  Renegade, PCBoard and friends running
  in DOS emulators (the DOSBox guide in
  this series is the tool). Archaeology,
  lovingly done — a different hobby from
  operating a living board.

## The heirs

Most of what BBSs did moved to the web,
and it is worth naming where, because your
callers will ask:

- **Forums** (Discourse and friends) took
  the message bases: threaded, searchable,
  public or private, in a browser. What
  they did not take is the door — a forum
  is a place you visit in a tab among
  forty; a BBS is a place you *go*.
- **Chat servers** (IRC then, Discord and
  Matrix now) took the live rooms, and
  group chats took the rest. They are
  better at presence and worse at memory:
  scrollback is not an archive with an
  index and a sysop who prunes it.
- **Usenet** — covered by its own guide in
  this series — is the message base idea
  at world scale, with no sysop and no
  door; read it beside this guide for the
  contrast between a community with an
  operator and a network with none.
- **Fediverse servers** (Mastodon and
  kin) took the small-community feeling
  and federated it — nearer to a networked
  BBS than anything else here, with the
  moderation ethics to match.

Synchronet's own answer to "why not those"
is that it can be several of them at once —
it speaks telnet, SSH, web, mail and
message-network protocols from one
program — while remaining, at heart, a
place with a door, a sysop, and four
nodes. If that sentence sounds like a
limitation, chapter 13 agrees with you in
places. If it sounds like the point, you
have found your software.
