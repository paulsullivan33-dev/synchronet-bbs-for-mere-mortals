**Outcome: you have read the one piece of

guide did not open — what is in them, and
what state the lab left them in.**

## The welcome mail

Synchronet writes to every new caller. The
moment Alex Rivers finished registering,
a message from the sysop account was
waiting, and the next login offered it
before anything else: "Read your unread
mail now?" The mail itself is one line —
"Sysop welcomes you to The Reading Room!"
— under a full header (transcript t09),
presented at a reading prompt:

```text
* Reading E-mail * (?=Menu) (1 of 1):
```

It is a small thing that teaches three
big ones. The board has **internal mail**
— caller-to-caller messages that are not
in any public board — distinct from the
public message bases of chapter 8. Mail
is offered **at logon**, before the menus,
so a caller cannot miss it. And the
sysop's name is on it: on a BBS, the
operator is a person callers hear from,
not a platform. From the main menu, `E`
opens the e-mail section — reading,
sending, and replying — at any time.

Internet e-mail is the mail server's job,
and this guide's board has that server
**off** (chapter 4). A board with it on
can exchange real e-mail with the world;
the configuration is the project's to
document, and this lab did not run it.

## The sections beyond the door

The main menu's "Go to" group names four
more sections. The lab visited none of
them beyond seeing their menu entries,
and this chapter describes them from the
project's documentation, labelled as such:

- **File Transfer section** (`T`) — file
  areas with uploads and downloads, the
  other great BBS institution beside
  messages. Callers transfer files with
  classic protocols (the `lrzsz` tools
  from chapter 3 provide ZMODEM). A
  private board can leave its file areas
  empty and lose nothing.
- **Text file section** (`G`) — a library
  of text files the sysop shelves for
  callers to read: rules, FAQs, documents.
- **Chat section** (`C`) — live chat
  between callers who are on at the same
  time, across nodes, plus pages to the
  sysop. On a four-node board this is a
  small room, and that is its charm.
- **External programs** (`X`) — **doors**:
  programs the board runs for a caller,
  historically games above all, but also
  utilities and entire applications.
  Doors are the BBS world's plugin
  system, with thirty years of software
  written for it — and thirty years of
  variation in quality and safety. The
  lab ran none, and a first board needs
  none.

Why devote a chapter to rooms the lab did
not enter? Because a sysop's first real
decision is which rooms to open, and the
menu shows callers the doors exist
whether you stock them or not. This
guide's board opens exactly one thing —
messages — plus the mail the board itself
sends. Everything else is present,
documented by the project, and knowingly
left for the day you have callers asking
for it.
